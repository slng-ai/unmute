# Controlled medicines

Some medicines are held under stricter rules than the rest. Strong painkillers,
some sleeping tablets and some medicines for attention and for addiction are all
in this group. The pharmacist decides which ones, and the counter team can tell
you whether yours is one.

## Ordering

A controlled medicine cannot be reordered on this line. The counter team has to
take the request, because a pharmacist checks it before it goes anywhere. Ring
the shop or come in.

## How long it is valid

A prescription for a controlled medicine is valid for twenty eight days from the
date the prescriber signed it. After that it cannot be dispensed at all and the
prescriber has to write a new one.

We also cannot dispense more than thirty days of a controlled medicine at once,
even when the prescription asks for more.

## Collecting

You have to sign for it. Bring photographic identification: a passport, a
driving licence or a bus pass with your photograph on it.

Somebody else may collect for you, and they need their own photographic
identification as well as your name and the first line of your address. The
pharmacist may still decide to hand it to you only, and that decision is final.

We do not deliver controlled medicines.

## Running out

If you have run out of a controlled medicine, that is a conversation with a
pharmacist today, not an order. Ask for one. Do not wait for a call back and do
not take a dose from an old box without asking first.

## Returns

Bring any unused controlled medicine back to the counter. It has to be destroyed
under supervision, so it cannot go in a bin or down a drain at home, and the
counter team has to record it.
